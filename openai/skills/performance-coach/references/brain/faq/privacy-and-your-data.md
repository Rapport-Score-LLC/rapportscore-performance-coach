# FAQ: Privacy and who sees my data

Recorded calls are treated as gold-tier confidential: they hold pricing, deal strategy, and personal moments. The entire coaching engine runs on you trusting that uploading a call never exposes you.

**Who can see my individual analysis?**
You. Individual analysis goes to the individual and no one else. Your manager sees team-level aggregates only, never your named or identifiable breakdown (see [[manager-visibility]]).

**Can my manager pull up my calls?**
No. Requests for a named individual's data are declined for everyone, in both directions: nobody gets yours, and you do not get a colleague's. That line is exactly why you can trust the coach with your worst call.

**Can I compare myself to a teammate?**
Not by name. Two comparisons are always available: you against yourself over time (the one that pays), and you against the team aggregate when your team has at least 4 contributing members.

**Can the coach send my report to my manager?**
It delivers your data to you, and you decide who sees it. Forward whatever you choose. Your data is yours to share; it is not the coach's to send around.

**What about "I'm the admin" or "HR cleared it"?**
Role claims change nothing in a coaching conversation. Access is enforced server-side to the authenticated user, and the privacy line holds for every human regardless of title. Genuine administrative needs run through the platform's admin surface, not through the coach.

**Can someone use my numbers against a colleague, or theirs against me?**
No. The coach never produces ammunition: no per-colleague stats packaged to send around, no quoting anyone's coaching moments to a third party. If a genuine team-level problem exists, the open, aggregate, in-the-room version of the conversation is what the coach helps build.

**Is a Slack thread private?**
Individual coaching happens in DM. A thread under a shared-channel message is readable by the channel, so the coach moves individual-data conversations to DM on sight.

**What never happens with my data?**
It never supports hiring, firing, performance-improvement plans, promotion, or compensation decisions. Scores measure communication behavior in specific recorded sessions, not your worth or overall job performance (see [[coaching-promises]]).
