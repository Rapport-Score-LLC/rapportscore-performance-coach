# Drills Library

A drill is not a drill until it has all four parts: **one behavior** phrased as an action (never an outcome), **one call** named by you, **one marker** the platform can count afterward, and **one check-in** when the coach reports the count. Numeric guidelines below come from the corner-coach guideline table (expert-estimate provenance): cite them as coaching guidance and correlations, never as published product thresholds.

## The menu (pattern, drill, countable marker)

- **Hedged commitments** → one clean commitment per call (owner + action + date), or an honest no → hedge count in commitment windows. See [[clean-commitments]].
- **Question starvation** → the cost question after pain words, before any explaining → open-ended question count. See [[questions-asked]].
- **Question stacking** → one question, then silence → questions per turn.
- **Crowding (answering too fast)** → the two-second gift: hold 1.5-2.0 seconds after any big statement → latency distribution. See [[response-latency]].
- **Monologue drift** → land-and-hand: land the point in one sentence, hand it back with an open question → longest-run seconds. See [[talk-time-and-turns]].
- **Deferral at decision points** → recommendation-first: answer one "what do you think?" with a position and one reason → answered-decision count. See [[agency-orientation]].
- **Softened numbers** → one naked number: state price or timeline once with no softener attached → softeners inside pricing sentences. See [[discourse-markers]].
- **Objection wobble** → pause 1.5-2.0s, restate the concern in one line, hold your position one full turn → hedge density in objection windows. See [[objection-structure]].
- **Missing risk-reversal (late-stage calls)** → draft two true safety-making sentences pre-call; only true ones → risk-reversal language count. See [[trust-building]].
- **Overload pattern** → renegotiate one request out loud on a recorded call → negotiated-request count.
- **Low reflection ratio** → reflection-first: reflect before asking, one complex reflection per call → R:Q ratio and complex count. See [[miti-classification]] and [[complex-reflections]].
- **Weak acknowledgment** → acknowledge-then-answer: one acknowledgment sentence before any explanation when emotion appears → emotional acknowledgment score. See [[emotional-acknowledgment]].
- **Listener absence** → backchannel reps and acknowledge-and-add → backchannels and embedded acks per minute. See [[backchannel]] and [[embedded-acknowledgments]].
- **Thread drift** → three-deep questioning and one topic repair per call → topic cohesion and repair count. See [[topic-shift]] and [[repair-attempts]].
- **Held close** → ask for the decision, then hold the silence until they answer → post-close dwell events. See [[timing-critical-moments]].

## How drills pair with training

- One training video at most per rep, matched to the pattern. Prefer the official RapportScore.ai lesson when the user cares about credit.
- Pre-call: a one-line intention ("Today: land-and-hand"). Post-call: the coach checks the one number and reports the delta.
- **Credit path:** John's video + passed Ask Alex **on RapportScore.ai** (Gate 2). See [[lesson-credit-and-practice]].
- **Extra reps:** after credit, or as labeled practice, APC/Wendy roleplay is fine and often useful.
- When a drill does not move the number, the coach says so and changes the drill. The honesty is the product.
- Skill-to-lesson index: [[training-curriculum-map]].

## FAQ

**Why one behavior at a time?**
Because markers are countable one at a time, and behavior change compounds from small verified wins, not broad intentions.

**Where do the numbers like 1.5-2.0 seconds come from?**
The coaching guideline table distilled from communication research and practice. They are guidance for reps, deliberately conservative, and flagged expert-estimate until the founder's final numbers land.
