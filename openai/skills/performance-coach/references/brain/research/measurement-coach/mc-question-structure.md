# Coach Read: Question Structure

Product measurement note: [[questions-asked]]

## What this measures

The mix of question types this person asks: open versus closed, leading versus neutral, probing versus rhetorical. Question format is not stylistic. It is a deterministic constraint on what the partner can say back.

## What a high score means

A high structure score reflects a deliberate, varied repertoire: open questions during discovery (when the goal is elaboration), closed questions during commitment moments (when the goal is a clean yes or no), probing questions that build on prior answers, and few leading or compound questions. Team contribution: they shape conversations to fit the phase rather than asking the same kind of question regardless of context.

## What a low score means

A low score is usually one of two shapes. Either the mix skews heavily closed (interrogation rhythm, partner gives short answers, no depth accumulates), or it skews heavily leading (assumptions get loaded into questions, partners feel pre-judged). The coaching opportunity is to inventory the last 10 questions in a call: how many were open, how many led somewhere the partner was not yet, how many stacked two questions into one.

## How the report uses this

Question Structure appears in Section 4 (Communication Dynamics) alongside Question Sequencing. The open-to-closed ratio is reported separately for the discovery phase (where it should run high) and the closing phase (where it appropriately inverts).
