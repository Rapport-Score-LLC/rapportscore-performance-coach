# Coach Read: Turn Taking

Product measurement note: [[talk-time-and-turns]]

## What this measures

Who speaks when, for how long, and how cleanly the conversation moves between turns: talk-time ratio, average turn length, gap duration at transitions, and the frequency and type of overlaps.

## What a high score means

A high score reflects smooth transitions (gaps in the 100-300 millisecond range), turn lengths that adapt to what the moment calls for, and a talk-time ratio that fits the call type. In discovery that is heavy on the partner's voice; in a presentation it is appropriately reversed. Team contribution: their conversations feel like conversations rather than alternating monologues.

## What a low score means

A low score usually surfaces as one of three patterns: extended gaps that create awkwardness, rigid turn lengths that ignore what the partner needs, or a talk-time ratio that runs heavily in one direction regardless of call type. The coaching opportunity is context-specific: identify the call type and the appropriate ratio, then watch a recording with the timer running to see where the imbalance accumulates.

## How the report uses this

Turn Taking appears in Section 4 (Communication Dynamics) and is one of the strongest inputs into Equity in Section 5. Read it next to Interruptions to separate balanced floor-sharing from competitive floor-claiming.
