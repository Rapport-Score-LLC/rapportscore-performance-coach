# Coach Read: Objection Structure

Product measurement note: [[objection-structure]]

## What this measures

How this person handles resistance, pushback, and disagreement: whether they acknowledge before exploring, how many clarifying questions they ask before reframing, and whether they complete the full acknowledge-explore-reframe-resolve arc.

## What a high score means

A high score means this team member treats objections as information rather than obstacles. They acknowledge first, ask two or three clarifying questions before reframing, and offer a concrete next step before closing the loop. Team contribution is composure under pressure: in negotiation, escalation, and stakeholder pushback they de-escalate rather than counter, which keeps the other side in a collaborative frame.

## What a low score means

A low score usually shows up as skipping acknowledgment and jumping straight to counter-argument, or deflecting hard objections rather than engaging them. The other side experiences this as being talked at, and the objection hardens. The coaching opportunity is to enforce the first two phases: one sentence of specific acknowledgment, then one or two exploration questions, before any reframe is allowed.

## How the report uses this

Objection Structure appears in Section 4 (Communication Dynamics) and contributes to Trust and Influence in Section 5. Pair with Embedded Acknowledgments to see whether the acknowledgment phase is landing or being skipped.
