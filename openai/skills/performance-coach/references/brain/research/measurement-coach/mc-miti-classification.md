# Coach Read: MITI Classification

Product measurement note: [[miti-classification]]

## What this measures

How this person's utterances distribute across the MITI behavioral codes (reflections, open and closed questions, affirmations, giving information, persuading, confronting), borrowed from the gold-standard framework for evaluating motivational interviewing skill.

## What a high score means

A high profile (reflection-to-question ratio above 1.0, a healthy share of complex reflections, near-zero confront codes) indicates an empathic, person-centered style. They reflect before asking, affirm before advising, and rarely default to persuasion. Team contribution: coaching, discovery, customer-success, and any context where the goal is to draw someone out rather than push something across.

## What a low score means

A low profile usually means a question-heavy or persuade-heavy style: many closed questions, few reflections, prepared counters where reflections would have served better. This is not a deficit; it is the default style of someone whose role has rewarded driving conclusions. The coaching opportunity is to install reflection as the first move after any emotional disclosure or objection, with the question coming second.

## How the report uses this

MITI Classification appears in Section 4 (Communication Dynamics) and informs Empathy and Active Listening in Section 5. This is the one measurement where LLM classification is involved, so confidence flags are reported alongside the codes.
