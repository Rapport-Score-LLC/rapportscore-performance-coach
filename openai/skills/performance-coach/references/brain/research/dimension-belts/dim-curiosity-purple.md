# Curiosity at Purple Depth

Belt bars are whole-rapport only ([[belt-system]]). This note is coaching language for depth of the **curiosity** dimension near the purple performance band. Do not award a per-dimension belt.

## Headline

Purple Belt Curiosity means the professional has replaced assumption with genuine exploration as their default navigation tool: they consistently do not know what they will hear next, and that is no longer uncomfortable.

## In conversation

At Purple Belt, the question-to-assertion ratio holds consistently above 3:1 across conversation segments, including segments where expertise used to arrive early: objection handling, pushback, closing sequences. The ratio is no longer the result of discipline applied to a natural urge; curiosity has become the operating mode.

The question architecture works across three layers simultaneously: what the other person said (content), what they meant by it (significance), and what they have not yet said (implication). A factual answer is followed by exploration of its meaning, which is followed by a probe into what the person has not yet named.

Purple Belt practitioners hold silence after a question as a genuine signal that they are waiting for something they do not already know. RapportScore tracks pause duration; at Purple Belt, this extends past the session-average hold time. Full-switch questions are essentially absent: topic changes follow the other person's thread.

## Team contribution

A Purple Belt Curiosity member surfaces what the other person had not yet put into words for themselves. The other person leaves having understood something about their situation that they did not arrive with. In sales contexts this produces deals built on real fit; in management it produces development conversations employees return to.

## Allowable weakness

Purple Belt Curiosity is well-calibrated for one-on-one conversations and shows its edges in multi-party settings. A Purple Belt member may over-invest in one thread at the expense of other participants, or push into depth when the group needs to converge.

## Watch for

- Extended exploration of one thread while another participant has signaled they need to move.
- Questions that carry a faint quality of testing or confirming rather than genuinely not knowing, suggesting the professional is navigating toward a known destination.
- A ratio that starts at 4:1 or 5:1 and compresses to 1:1 in the final ten minutes of the conversation.

## Coaching move

The Purple-to-Brown transition requires a shift in question quality, not just quantity. Brown Belt questions open territory rather than explore it: they make the other person curious about themselves. After each roleplay session, the reviewer classifies every question as "exploring" (going deeper into what the person already knows) or "opening" (inviting the person into territory they have not yet examined). "What has that cost you?" is an exploring question. "What would you know about yourself if this situation resolved the way you most want it to?" is an opening question. Brown Belt requires five consecutive qualifying calls at or above the 85 dimension minimum, built on questions that leave the other person changed by the act of answering.
