# Emotional Acknowledgment at Purple Depth

Belt bars are whole-rapport only ([[belt-system]]). This note is coaching language for depth of the **emotional acknowledgment** dimension near the purple performance band. Do not award a per-dimension belt.

## Headline

Purple Belt Emotional Acknowledgment means the practitioner holds the emotional architecture of the conversation without losing a thread, and their acknowledgment creates space the other person actually uses.

## In conversation

At Purple Belt, the distinguishing behavior is not that acknowledgment happens but what it opens. The practitioner names both the declared emotion and the one beneath it in a single move ("I can hear the frustration with the timeline, and I'm also picking up something about whether the recommendation itself feels right to you"). RapportScore measures whether these layered acknowledgments register as distinct signals in sentiment analysis; at Purple they do.

Acknowledgment timing is consistently followed by a deliberate hold. This response latency gap is measurable in the transcript, and the other person's next turn is longer than after a Blue acknowledgment. Talk share in high-emotion turns is lower than the session average, making the hold structural rather than passive. Acknowledgment-to-bypass ratio is high for clear signals and materially improved for subtle ones. The practitioner tracks multiple threads and returns to concerns named early but set aside ("We touched on the timeline concern earlier; I want to make sure we didn't leave that unresolved").

## Team contribution

A Purple Belt member is why people leave conversations feeling understood rather than merely heard. In complex conversations this means the other person shares what is actually blocking them, not the version they present to everyone else. This practitioner makes it safe for peers to surface reservations they cannot fully articulate.

## Allowable weakness

Purple Belt does not yet produce the arc-level effect that characterizes Brown: the other person does not leave conversations saying things they came in not planning to say as a reliable pattern. In low-content conversations, attention to the emotional dimension can feel slightly effortful, and the seam between technique and instinct is still faintly visible.

## Watch for

- Layered acknowledgments followed immediately by the practitioner's own content without a pause.
- The same emotional thread surfacing three or more times without feeling fully resolved.
- An emotional arc ending at roughly the same sentiment level it started at.

## Coaching move

The Purple-to-Brown transition is about letting acknowledgment become structural rather than deliberate. After each recorded call, map the other party's emotional trajectory: where sentiment shifted, when those shifts were acknowledged, whether acknowledgment landed, and whether threads were left open at close. The roleplay session should present a scenario where the buyer's stated concern and actual concern diverge; the passing condition is surfacing the unstated one without being told it is there. Sustaining an acknowledgment-to-bypass ratio at or above 0.85 across three to five consecutive calls is the Gate 3 evidence for Brown readiness; the behavioral marker is whether the other person said something they would not have said to a Blue Belt counterpart.
