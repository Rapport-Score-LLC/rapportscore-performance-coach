# DISC Style: SD / Steady-Driver

Session-observed communication style guidance. Not personality destiny. People flex across contexts. Verify across multiple interactions.

## Headline

An SD holds the room steady and then, when the moment requires it, moves it. They are the team member most likely to be quiet for most of a meeting and then own the closing decision.

## In conversation

SD speakers carry a Steady's overall talk share profile (at or below the team mean, flat across the meeting) but with a single late-meeting spike that a pure S would not produce. Response latency is long throughout but drops sharply at the close; the D-secondary appears as a sudden acceleration rather than a constant. Question architecture is mostly open and confirming, with closed and challenge forms surfacing only in the final third: "are we agreed on this," "who owns the next step." Interruption profile is cooperative-dominant and low-rate, but the D-secondary licenses one or two well-placed competitive interruptions at the close. Lexical entrainment (LSM) is high and stable across the call. Topic-shift authorship is low; SD speakers do not change topics, they advance the existing one when they judge the timing right. Repair attempts are deliberate and often the most effective in the room.

## Team contribution

SD speakers are the team's quiet closers. They are most valuable in long-cycle internal decisions, executive standing meetings, and any conversation where the room needs to feel held before it will accept a commitment. Without an SD, teams with strong S-energy often hold the relationship and avoid the close; teams with strong D-energy often force the close at a cost to the relationship.

## Allowable weakness

SD speakers can hold the room past the moment a decision was needed, waiting for a timing that never quite arrives. The S-primary can also keep them silent during a window where the D-secondary would have been useful earlier, leaving the team to navigate without their stabilizing closing pressure.

## Watch for

- An SD whose late-meeting D-spike does not appear. Often means the moment for the close passed and the SD did not name it.
- Response latency stretching across the entire meeting. Sometimes the SD is processing rather than holding.
- Repair attempts to ruptures the team caused while the SD waited. Their patience can read as endorsement.

## Coaching move

Introduce a name-the-window practice. At the start of every meeting, the SD names the moment by which a decision needs to land ("by minute forty we need the owner on this"). Watch the next session for whether they then close the room at that moment rather than past it. If they do, the holding and the closing are both being used.
