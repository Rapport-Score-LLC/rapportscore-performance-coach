# DISC Style: SI / Steady-Influencer

Session-observed communication style guidance. Not personality destiny. People flex across contexts. Verify across multiple interactions.

## Headline

An SI holds the room steady and warms it without ever raising the temperature. They are the team member most likely to be the one a difficult teammate trusts first.

## In conversation

SI speakers carry a Steady's overall profile (flat talk share, long latency, low interruption rate) layered with the relational signal of an Influencer. Response latency stays long but the responses themselves carry more affiliative content than a pure S would produce; small recognition markers ("that makes sense, thank you for raising it"), warmth in the diction, and quiet enthusiasm rather than active enthusiasm. Question architecture is open and confirming throughout, with affiliative forms more frequent than a pure S would use ("how are you holding up with this," "what is making this one hard"). Closed and challenge forms are very rare. Interruption profile is almost entirely cooperative, low-rate, and warmer in framing than either pure S or pure I. Lexical entrainment (LSM) is very high and the slowest to develop, but the deepest once locked in. Repair attempts are frequent, gentle, and often pre-emptive.

## Team contribution

SI speakers are the team's safety anchor. They are most valuable in conflict, in change windows, and in any conversation with a teammate or counterpart who needs to feel both held and welcomed. Without an SI, teams often handle conflict by avoidance and lose the trust currency that the SI quietly generates in every interaction.

## Allowable weakness

SI speakers very rarely close, and they can absorb friction so successfully that the team never learns that friction was there. The same warmth that holds the room can leave a decision unmade and an unmet expectation unnamed. They can also under-advocate for themselves, deferring credit to others in moments where naming their own contribution would have served the team.

## Watch for

- An SI whose meetings consistently end with a softer next step than the situation required. The warmth has carried past the commitment.
- Repair attempts to ruptures the SI noticed before anyone else did. They may be doing the team's emotional accounting alone.
- Talk share staying flat when an inflection was needed. The room felt good and went home undecided.

## Coaching move

Introduce a name-your-position practice. Before the close of any meeting, the SI states their own recommendation in one sentence rather than reflecting the room's. Watch the next session for whether the team responds to that recommendation directly rather than smoothing past it. If they do, the SI's judgment is being heard and not just their warmth.
