# Pair Dynamic: Influencer and Steady

How two session-measured styles tend to interact. Anchor claims to pair LSM, latency asymmetry, interruption direction, and repair rates when data exists.

## Headline

An Influencer and a Steady combine energy and grounding: the I lifts the room, the S keeps it safe. When functional, this pair produces the team's warmest and most psychologically generous conversations.

## What this pairing looks like

An Influencer (I) and a Steady (S) share a people-first orientation; both prioritize relationships and both read the emotional temperature of the room well. The I brings enthusiasm and momentum, the S brings patience and continuity. In practice this pair creates a feeling of ease and welcome that makes it the natural home for onboarding, recovery, and mentoring work. The gap shows up when the conversation needs to hold a hard line rather than hold a relationship.

## Team contribution
This pair is highest-leverage on onboarding, mentoring, peer-support conversations, customer-care recovery, team retrospectives that need both energy and trust, and any session where a teammate or counterpart is processing something difficult and needs to feel both seen and warmed. They are the trust and rapport infrastructure of the team. Without an I-S pair, teams can hold high technical capacity and quietly lose the relational fabric that holds it together.

## Allowable weakness
This pair will struggle with conflict that needs to be surfaced rather than smoothed, with closing work that requires holding the room past comfortable, and with conversations that need to disappoint someone in real time. Both members lean toward conflict avoidance by default, the I through redirection and the S through absorption, and left alone with a hard conversation they will route around it.

## In conversation

Pair-level LSM is high and builds quickly; both members adapt to each other, with the I accelerating the entrainment and the S sustaining it. Pair-level response latency is split early (I fast, S slower) and converges as the conversation settles, with the I lengthening pauses to match the S's rhythm. Interruption directionality is bilateral and almost entirely cooperative; competitive interruptions are very rare. Repair-attempt frequency is the highest of any pair on the matrix; both members detect rupture quickly, both move to repair it, and they sometimes repair the same rupture from two sides. Turn-taking balance is symmetric, with the I holding slightly more talk share. Topic-shift authorship is asymmetric: the I leads shifts while the S anchors back to the current topic. Backchannel density is elevated on both sides and is one of the pair's most distinguishing features; mutual affirmation is frequent, warm, and largely synchronized, which produces high felt safety and can slow the pair's willingness to surface friction. Pair_safety_score is the highest of any pair on the matrix alongside the C-S pair. Compatibility_score is strong on care work and weak on conflict work.

## Watch for

- Repair attempts climbing across a session without the underlying friction being named. The pair is smoothing the symptom while the cause stays in the room.
- A decision that needed pushback being passed forward as agreement. The pair valued the warmth over the necessary friction.
- Pair_safety_score elevated alongside an external commitment that quietly fails the following week. The conversation felt good and did not commit.

## Coaching move
When the pair has to deliver hard feedback or hold a hard line, agree before the session on the one sentence that has to be said as written, no softening. Either member can deliver it; the rule is that the sentence comes out of the meeting unmodified. Watch the next session for whether the sentence was delivered and whether the relationship survived it. If both, the pair has converted warmth into the safety container that lets hard truth land instead of substituting for it.
