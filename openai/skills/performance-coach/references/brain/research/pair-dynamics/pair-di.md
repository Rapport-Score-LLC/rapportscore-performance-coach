# Pair Dynamic: Driver and Influencer

How two session-measured styles tend to interact. Anchor claims to pair LSM, latency asymmetry, interruption direction, and repair rates when data exists.

## Headline

A Driver and an Influencer land the call and win the room in the same conversation. When functional, this pair is the team's external-facing closing engine.

## What this pairing looks like

A Driver (D) and an Influencer (I) are the two highest-energy styles on the DISC matrix and they amplify each other in both directions. The I builds trust and energy fast; the D converts that trust into a named commitment. In practice this pair looks confident, fast, and persuasive. They take up space in a room and they close things. The risk shows up when the room needed more air than the pair left it.

## Team contribution
This pair is highest-leverage on high-stakes external sessions: new-relationship deals, board meetings, recruiting closes, and any conversation where a stranger has to be turned into a willing collaborator and a named commitment in the same hour. The I opens the door, the D closes it, and the counterpart leaves with both a feeling and an obligation.

## Allowable weakness
This pair will struggle with conversations that need quiet teammates to surface concerns, with internal team-building work where the rest of the team needs floor time, and with deliberate slow-paced rigor work where the speed of the pair erodes the quality of the inputs. They will also struggle to notice when a counterpart is uncomfortable; the I will read the surface signals as fine, the D will move past them, and the deal can be agreed to in a register the counterpart will later regret.

## In conversation

Pair-level LSM is asymmetric and high in one direction; the I mirrors the D's diction quickly, and the D rarely loosens to match the I. Pair-level response latency is short on both sides and the latencies converge toward the I's rhythm in the opening third and toward the D's rhythm in the closing third. Interruption directionality is bilateral and heavily cooperative; the I finishes the D's sentences to seal the rapport, the D finishes the I's sentences to seal the commitment. Repair-attempt frequency is moderate and weighted toward the I. Turn-taking balance often climbs above the team mean for the pair combined; rs_network_scores will frequently show this pair holding a disproportionate share of session airtime. Topic-shift authorship is concentrated on the I side, with the D using the shifts as runway toward the next commitment. Backchannel output from the I is high and functions as social lubrication; the D's backchannel rate is low, keeping forward momentum the dominant register. Pair_safety_score is high externally and softer internally. Compatibility_score is strong on closing work.

## Watch for

- Combined pair talk share above sixty percent in any session where more than three teammates are present. The other voices are being crowded out.
- Cooperative interruption rate from I to D falling late in the meeting. Often a tell that the I has lost the room and is no longer scaffolding the close.
- A counterpart agreement that softens in follow-up. The pair closed something the counterpart had not fully processed.

## Coaching move
Before any high-stakes session, the pair agrees on one open question they will hand to the room in the second quarter of the meeting, and the I owns asking it. Watch the next session for whether the question gets asked and whether the pair holds silence for at least three seconds after. If they do, the warmth and the velocity have bought air for the rest of the room instead of consuming it.
