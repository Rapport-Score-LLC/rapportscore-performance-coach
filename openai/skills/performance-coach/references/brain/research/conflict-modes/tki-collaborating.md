# Conflict Mode: Collaborating

Observed approach to disagreement in session. Situational tool, not character label.

## Headline

A Collaborating mode is the team's high-investment option. It is the right call when the problem is genuinely complex and both parties' contribution is needed for the solution to hold, and a poor default when the issue does not warrant the time.

## In conversation

Collaborating produces the most balanced conversational signature of the five modes. Talk share between the two parties converges toward parity over the course of the exchange. Question architecture skews open and exploratory: "what would have to be true for this to work for you," "what am I missing about your constraint." Response latency is moderate to long; both speakers are thinking, not reacting. Topic-shift authorship is shared rather than concentrated. Interruptions, when present, are cooperative and almost always backed by an acknowledgement token in the next turn. The strongest assess is repair-attempt frequency: in a true Collaborating exchange, repair attempts are high and almost always succeed, because both parties are actively working to make the other understood. Counterpart-acknowledgement language ("what I am hearing is," "say more about") runs well above the team baseline.

## Team contribution

This mode is highest-leverage on cross-functional decisions, on conflicts where both parties will own the outcome, and on any problem where the cost of a partial solution is higher than the cost of the meeting itself. It is also the mode that builds durable trust; a team that has Collaborated once together negotiates faster the next time.

## Allowable weakness

Collaborating is expensive. It takes time, attention, and willingness from both sides, and not every conflict justifies it. Used as a default it produces meeting overhead, decision delay, and a team that cannot triage low-stakes disagreements without convening. The same depth that makes it powerful makes it the wrong tool for trivial calls.

## Watch for

- Collaborating attempts where one party has already decided. Signature shows talk-share parity and high acknowledgement language from one side but zero topic-shift authorship from the other. The exchange looks collaborative and is actually a slow Competing.
- Collaborating extended past the point of new information. Repair attempts continue, talk share stays balanced, but no new content enters the conversation. The team is processing, not deciding.
- A team that defaults to Collaborating on every conflict. Watch meeting volume rise without decision velocity rising with it.

## Coaching move

Prescribe a two-question opener for any conflict the consultant flags as collaboration-worthy. Both parties answer, out loud, before the substantive conversation begins: "What outcome do you need from this conversation to count it as resolved?" and "What is one constraint of mine you do not yet fully understand?" The first surfaces the actual stakes; the second forces the room to acknowledge that the other side has information the first side lacks. Observe in the next session whether the opener is used and whether repair attempts succeed on the first try rather than the third.
