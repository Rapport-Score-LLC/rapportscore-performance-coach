# Conflict Mode: Avoiding

Observed approach to disagreement in session. Situational tool, not character label.

## Headline

An Avoiding mode is the team's deferral lever. It is the right call when the issue is genuinely trivial, the timing is wrong, or someone needs to cool off, and it is corrosive when it becomes the way the team handles anything uncomfortable.

## In conversation

Avoiding produces the quietest signature of the five modes. The avoiding speaker's talk share drops well below their personal baseline during the conflict window. Response latency rises; pauses lengthen before they speak, and when they do speak the turn is short. Question architecture shifts toward deflection and procedural framing: "can we come back to this," "this might be one for another time," "I don't have enough context to weigh in." Topic-shift authorship is the strongest tell. The avoiding speaker introduces an off-topic shift shortly after the conflict surfaces, often a return to a safer agenda item. Counterpart-acknowledgement language drops or vanishes; the avoiding party stops naming what the other side said. Interruption frequency falls toward zero from that speaker, and repair attempts directed at them by others go unanswered or get a one-token reply.

## Team contribution

This mode is highest-leverage when the issue genuinely does not warrant the conversation: a trivial preference disagreement, a minor process gripe, a topic the team has revisited five times without new information. It is also the right call when emotions are running hot enough that any substantive exchange will produce damage the team will spend the next week repairing. A skilled avoider buys the team time to come back to the issue when conditions are better.

## Allowable weakness

Avoiding never resolves anything; it postpones. That cost is bearable for trivial issues and intolerable for substantive ones. Chronic avoidance is the single biggest predictor of low psychological-safety scores at the team level, because the people doing the avoiding eventually stop bringing anything difficult to the table at all.

## Watch for

- Avoiding from the same speaker across multiple substantive topics. This is not a mode choice anymore; it is a signal that the speaker does not feel safe contributing under conflict.
- A topic that gets deferred twice and then never re-surfaces. Track topic-shift authorship session over session; recurring deflection-and-drop on the same theme is structural avoidance.
- A team-wide pattern of short response latencies on safe topics and long ones on hard topics. The team has learned which conversations to skip.

## Coaching move

Prescribe a "parking lot with an owner and a date" practice. Whenever a topic is deferred, the consultant requires the team to name three things out loud before the topic is allowed to drop: who owns re-opening it, what new information would make the next conversation more productive, and when (session date, not "soon") it will be re-opened. The structure makes the difference between strategic deferral and chronic avoidance visible. Topics that get owners and dates are genuine timing calls; topics that resist getting owners and dates are the ones the team is avoiding. Observe in the next two sessions whether any parked items return on their named date.
