# Power Dynamics

## What it measures

Subtle indicators of conversational control and influence: interruption frequency and direction (who interrupts whom), response latency (who waits for whom), and topic control (who sets the agenda). The session network view adds alignment flows: how much each speaker entrained toward others (Align Out, the "joining" work) versus how much others entrained toward them (Align In, the social-influence signal), summed as centrality (who anchored the room). Research anchor: language effects and power differences in social interaction, the "Echoes of Power" line of research (Danescu-Niculescu-Mizil et al. 2012).

## Where you see it

The Power Dynamics card (Deep Dive tab) and the Conversation Network Signature card (per-speaker centrality, Align In and Align Out, interruption counts both directions).

## How to read your number

- No single "good" reading: match the dynamics to your goal. In sales, letting the client hold more control is often right; in leadership conversations, balance usually serves best.
- Interruption reads: higher interruptions are not always bad (they can show engagement); a consistent one-way interrupter is the dominant speaker. Reduce interruptions in high-stakes moments.
- Latency reads: fast responses signal high engagement or impatience; slow responses signal thoughtfulness or distance.
- Network reads: highest centrality means you anchored the room, which is neutral information (appropriate if facilitating, a flag if dominating). High Align In with low Align Out marks the conversational gravity well, often the prospect when engaged. High Align Out with low Align In is the joining work, healthy for a seller in discovery.
- Talk time is volume; centrality is influence. Someone can anchor a room with moderate talk time if their style is influential.

## What moves it

- Interruption discipline and floor-holding (see [[interruptions]]).
- Deliberate agenda moves: introducing topics, reframing objections, closing loops (see [[agency-orientation]]).
- The joining work early: adapting your style in the first five minutes raises the room's alignment toward the goal (see [[entrainment]]).
- Over-accommodation shows here too: if you are the style adopter in every pairing, practice holding your linguistic ground.

## Drills

- One deliberate agenda move per call: open with the frame ("here's what I'd like to cover; what would you add?"). See [[drills-library]].
- The first-five-minutes joining rep in discovery calls: match pace and vocabulary before making any ask.

## FAQ

**Is dominating a call ever right?**
Control should fit the moment: a demo you lead is not dominance. The flag is control the conversation type did not call for, or control the other side visibly stopped consenting to.

**Can I see who deferred to whom?**
The response-latency pair table and interruption direction give the observable version of deference. The measurement stays behavioral; it never labels anyone's character.
