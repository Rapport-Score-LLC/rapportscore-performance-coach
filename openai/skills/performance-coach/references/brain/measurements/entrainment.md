# Entrainment

## What it measures

A composite of linguistic synchrony with your conversation partner: how much your vocabulary, rhythm, and function-word usage converged. The composite blends four components: an LSM component (see [[language-style-matching]]), a content-adoption component (whether your partner's terms get taken up), a syntactic component (whether grammatical structures get mirrored), and a hedge-convergence component (whether certainty levels moved together across the call). When LSM data is unavailable, a fallback formula uses the remaining components. Research anchor: the mechanistic psychology of dialogue and interactive alignment (Pickering and Garrod 2004).

## Where you see it

The Cross-Session Entrainment Trend chart (your score across recent sessions), the Sync Score card (which combines verbal matching, interaction rhythm, and pace synchronization), and the full component breakdown card for a single session.

## How to read your number

- Cross-session chart: each point is one session's composite. The trend line is the headline: rising means your sessions are becoming more synchronized.
- The chart's score bands (above 65 strong, below 35 weak) are a working internal heuristic under review, not a validated threshold. Treat them as orientation only.
- High variance (a spiky chart) means your alignment is context-dependent: you synchronize well with some people and not others. A flat low trend is a consistent accommodation gap, the pattern most worth coaching.
- Component reading: the lowest component tile is your coaching target. A falling LSM trend late in a call means divergence after a good start. Hedge convergence moving together signals shared certainty alignment; diverging means one side grew more assertive while the other hedged more.
- Multi-party sessions are represented by the strongest pair.

## What moves it

- Function-word mirroring: adopt "we/us" versus "I/you" framing when the other person uses it.
- Matching sentence complexity: if they speak in short declaratives, reduce your clause nesting.
- Pausing briefly before responding: rushed responses lower entrainment by skipping the listening phase.
- Entrainment is described in the product as predicting rapport better than individual turns: it is the accumulated effect of hundreds of micro-adjustments.

## Drills

- Component-targeted rep: read your component breakdown, pick the lowest, and run its drill for one call (pronoun mirroring for LSM, echo-as-question for content adoption, one breath before responding for rhythm). See [[drills-library]].

## FAQ

**Is high entrainment always good?**
Mutual convergence is the strong rapport signal. If you are always the one adapting in every pairing, that is over-accommodation, worth balancing by holding your own framing sometimes (see [[power-dynamics]]).

**Why did a great call score moderate?**
Demos and pitch-shaped calls have structural divergence built in; the applicability logic accounts for call shape. Check the conversation type before reading the number.
