# Counterpart Reciprocity

## What it measures

How much the other side matched your conversational moves: whether your questions, disclosures, and invitations were met with theirs. Scored 0 to 100 (note: the scale is 0-100 even though the underlying construct is a ratio-style index). Research anchor: adaptation in dyadic interaction, defining and operationalizing interpersonal adaptation (Burgoon, Dillman and Stern 1993).

## Where you see it

The Reciprocity tile on the Signal Stack (Deep Dive tab).

## How to read your number

- Direction: higher means the counterpart is actively co-investing in the conversation.
- Signal Stack thresholds (exploratory v1 defaults, not calibrated against outcomes): 70 or above means active co-investment; below 40 suggests one-sided effort.
- Combined reads from the card: high reciprocity with low trust language reads as engaged but skeptical; low reciprocity with building trust reads as warming up but not yet active (see [[trust-indicators]]).
- Track it across sessions with the same counterpart: a rising reciprocity trend is one of the clearest relationship-progress signals the stack offers.

## What moves it

- You cannot control their moves, but you can shape the invitations: open questions invite answers, disclosures invite disclosures, and handing the floor back gives reciprocity room to happen.
- Low reciprocity with high talk time on your side usually means there was no space to reciprocate into (see [[talk-time-and-turns]]).
- Matching their pace and style first (the joining work) tends to precede them matching your moves (see [[entrainment]]).

## Drills

- Disclosure-for-disclosure: share one relevant, honest piece of context early, then ask an open question, and watch whether the reciprocity read moves next session. See [[drills-library]].

## FAQ

**Their reciprocity is low. Did I fail?**
Not necessarily. It is a read on their observed engagement, which you influence but do not control. The coachable side is whether your moves left room and invitation for theirs.

**Is a one-sided call always bad?**
Some call types are structurally one-sided (a briefing, a demo). Read the number against the conversation type before treating it as a relationship signal.
