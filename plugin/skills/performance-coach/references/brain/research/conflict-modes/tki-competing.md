# Conflict Mode: Competing

Observed approach to disagreement in session. Situational tool, not character label.

## Headline

A Competing mode is the team's emergency lever. It is the right call when the cost of delay is higher than the cost of disagreement, and a costly default when it is not.

## In conversation

Competing shows up as high talk share concentrated in the speaker driving the position, paired with low response latency and a question architecture that skews toward closed and rhetorical: "are we doing this or not," "what is the alternative you are proposing." Topic-shift authorship is low; the Competing speaker is not changing the subject, they are forcing closure on the current one. Interruption patterns are the assess signal. In an actual emergency, interruptions read as cooperative and the room cedes airtime willingly. Outside an emergency, the same interruption count reads as competitive: the other speakers' turn-completion rates drop, repair-attempt frequency from the non-competing parties rises (they keep restarting sentences that got run over), and counterpart-acknowledgement language falls toward zero. Response latency from everyone else lengthens within two or three turns as the room recalibrates to a one-voice conversation.

## Team contribution

This mode is highest-leverage in genuine crises, in safety-critical calls, and in the closing minutes of a negotiation where a decision must land. It is also the right mode when a value is non-negotiable and clarity matters more than buy-in. Without someone capable of competing in those windows, teams stall on calls that needed to be made an hour ago.

## Allowable weakness

Competing is by design low-cooperative. The person using it is choosing the outcome over the relationship in that moment. The cost is real: people who get over-ruled remember it, and the next conversation starts from a lower trust baseline than the last one ended on.

## Watch for

- Competing as a default mode across non-urgent topics. Trust erodes in inches; nobody flags it at the time.
- Competing paired with falling counterpart-acknowledgement language. The other side has stopped trying to be heard.
- A rise in competitive (not cooperative) interruptions session over session. This is a stress signal, not a personality fact.

## Coaching move

Prescribe a "name the mode out loud" practice for the next session. Before pushing a Competing close, the speaker says one sentence: "I am going to push for a decision here because the cost of waiting is higher than the cost of being wrong." The sentence does two things: it tells the room this is a deliberate mode choice rather than a default, and it gives anyone in the room standing to challenge the urgency premise if they disagree. Observe in the following session whether the speaker uses the sentence and whether competitive interruptions drop.
