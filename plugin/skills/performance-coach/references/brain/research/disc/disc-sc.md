# DISC Style: SC / Steady-Conscientious

Session-observed communication style guidance. Not personality destiny. People flex across contexts. Verify across multiple interactions.

## Headline

An SC holds the team steady and protects it from the under-considered decision. They are the team member most likely to be both the calmest voice and the most prepared voice in the room.

## In conversation

SC speakers carry a Steady's overall profile (low talk share, long latency, low interruption rate) layered with a Conscientious's density per turn. When they speak, their contributions are dense, structured, and well-sourced. Question architecture is open and confirming early, with probing forms ("what is the assumption underneath this," "how was this defined") appearing in the middle of the meeting. Closed and challenge forms are very rare; SC speakers test by question, not by pressure. Interruption profile is almost entirely absent; the few that occur are cooperative and clarifying. Lexical entrainment (LSM) is moderate; the SC adapts pace and register but holds diction precise, similar to a pure C. Topic-shift authorship is low; they go deeper, not sideways. Repair attempts are infrequent and deliberate, often offered as a structured reframe rather than a warm gesture.

## Team contribution

SC speakers are the team's reliable institutional memory. They are most valuable in long-cycle decisions, post-mortem reviews, and any team where the cost of repeating a prior mistake is high. They are also the most patient teacher on the team. Without an SC, teams often lose the slow, careful synthesis that prevents the same flawed assumption from surfacing every quarter.

## Allowable weakness

SC speakers can be slow to commit, slower to change once committed, and difficult to move when the situation requires speed. The S-primary and C-secondary reinforce each other in the direction of caution. They can also withhold their assessment until they are certain, by which point the team has already committed to a path the SC could have improved.

## Watch for

- An SC whose probing questions consistently arrive after the team has committed. Their highest-leverage contribution is going unused.
- Response latency stretching past the personal baseline. Usually means the meeting is moving faster than their processing window.
- A meeting where the SC does not speak. Almost always a signal that the team has skipped a pressure-test the SC would have provided.

## Coaching move

Introduce a name-the-half-formed-thought practice. The SC contributes one observation before they feel ready, framed explicitly as half-formed. Watch the next session for whether the team treats the half-formed observation as useful rather than as incomplete. If they do, the SC's slow synthesis is being captured live rather than after the decision was made.
