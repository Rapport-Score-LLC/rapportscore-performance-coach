# DISC Style: Pure D / Driver

Session-observed communication style guidance. Not personality destiny. People flex across contexts. Verify across multiple interactions.

## Headline

A pure Driver moves a conversation toward a decision faster than anyone else in the room. They are the team member most likely to convert a thirty minute discussion into a two minute commitment.

## In conversation

Drivers typically run above the team mean on talk share during decision windows, even when their overall talk share is closer to par. Response latency is short; they respond before the prior speaker has fully released the floor. Their question architecture skews heavily toward closed and challenge forms: "is this the decision," "who owns it," "by when." Open and probing questions are scarce. Interruption profile leans competitive rather than cooperative, and the rate climbs as the meeting approaches its close. Topic-shift authorship is moderate but consequential; Drivers rarely change topics for novelty, they cut to the next item on the path. Lexical entrainment (LSM) with counterparts is low; Drivers expect others to match them, not the other way around. Repair attempts are infrequent because a Driver usually does not register the rupture in real time.

## Team contribution

Drivers are most valuable in the closing third of a meeting, in crises, and in any conversation where the cost of indecision exceeds the cost of a wrong call. They convert directional consensus into named ownership and named deadlines. Without a Driver present, teams routinely leave conversations with implicit agreements that quietly fail to land.

## Allowable weakness

Drivers can come across as blunt, impatient, and indifferent to emotional context. The same low-friction directness that closes a deal can flatten a teammate who needed thirty more seconds to surface a concern. Their tolerance for ambiguity is low by design.

## Watch for

- A Driver paired with a high-S counterpart who has gone silent. The Driver hears alignment; the S has withdrawn.
- Competitive interruption rate rising mid-meeting. Usually a stress signal that something upstream feels unsafe.
- A Driver opening discovery calls. The first quarter of a meeting is rarely their highest-leverage window.

## Coaching move

Before any meeting where the Driver is not the decision owner, have them write down the one question they will not answer themselves: the question they will hand to the room. Watch the next session for whether that question gets asked before minute ten. If it does, the Driver has bought the team enough air to surface what the Driver would otherwise have closed off.
