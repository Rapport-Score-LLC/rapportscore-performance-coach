# DISC Style: DI / Driver-Influencer

Session-observed communication style guidance. Not personality destiny. People flex across contexts. Verify across multiple interactions.

## Headline

A DI lands the call and wins the room in the same sentence. They are the team member most likely to close a decision while the counterpart is still smiling about the story they told two minutes ago.

## In conversation

DI speakers carry the talk share profile of a Driver (high during decision windows) but with the early-meeting energy curve of an Influencer. Response latency on rapport moments is faster than a pure D's; the I-secondary makes them lean into laughter, recognition, and shared experience markers rather than push past them. Question architecture is a hybrid: closed and challenge forms at the close, but the open and affiliative forms appear in the first third where a pure D would skip them. Interruption profile shifts noticeably toward cooperative compared with a pure D, especially in the opening; competitive interruptions still appear late in the meeting when the close is being negotiated. Topic-shift authorship is higher than a pure D's, often using a story or analogy to reroute the room toward the commitment. Lexical entrainment (LSM) is moderate, higher than a pure D's, and the DI uses it strategically rather than instinctively.

## Team contribution

DI speakers are the team's high-stakes external face. They are most valuable in deals where the decision is large, the relationship is new, and both have to be won in the same session. Without a DI present, teams that have a strong D often close deals on terms that work logically and feel transactional to the counterpart.

## Allowable weakness

DI speakers can overcommit to relational warmth they then close past, leaving the counterpart with a small private feeling that they were charmed into the decision. The same energy that opens the room can blur the line between persuasion and pressure. They can also under-document; the relationship feels like enough until it stops being enough.

## Watch for

- A DI whose cooperative interruption rate flattens late in the meeting. Often a tell that the warmth was scaffolding, not connection.
- Talk share climbing across the meeting rather than tapering. The room is being driven, not led.
- Repair attempts the DI skips because the close is already in motion.

## Coaching move

Introduce a pause-before-close practice. Before naming the commitment, the DI asks one open question and waits a full three seconds before continuing. Watch the next session for whether the counterpart fills the silence with something the DI did not script. If they do, the deal is being agreed to rather than carried.
