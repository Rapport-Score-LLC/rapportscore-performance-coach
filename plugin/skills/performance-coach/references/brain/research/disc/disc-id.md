# DISC Style: ID / Influencer-Driver

Session-observed communication style guidance. Not personality destiny. People flex across contexts. Verify across multiple interactions.

## Headline

An ID wins the room and then asks for the commitment before the warmth fades. They are the team member most likely to convert relational energy into a signed next step inside the same call.

## In conversation

ID speakers run an Influencer's opening profile (high talk share, very short latency, dense cooperative interruptions) and add a Driver's closing discipline. Question architecture is mostly open and affiliative in the first half of the meeting, then shifts noticeably toward closed and challenge forms at the close: "are we doing this," "by when." Topic-shift authorship is high throughout, often using stories and analogies, but the late-meeting shifts are oriented toward commitment rather than connection. Interruption profile is cooperative-dominant early and mixed late; the D-secondary licenses competitive interruptions at the close that a pure I would have softened. Lexical entrainment (LSM) is high and the ID uses it actively as a closing tool, not just as a connection tool. Repair attempts are quick, warm, and frequent throughout.

## Team contribution

ID speakers are the team's relationship-led closers. They are most valuable in deals where the counterpart needs to feel chosen before they will commit, and in any conversation where the team's brand is "the warm one that still ships." Without an ID, teams with strong I-energy often build the relationship and leave the deal unclosed; teams with strong D-energy often close the deal and miss the next three the relationship would have produced.

## Allowable weakness

ID speakers can confuse rapport with consent. The warmth carries the room far enough that the close lands easily, and the counterpart can later discover they agreed to more than they intended to. The D-secondary can also surface unexpectedly mid-story, leaving the room with a small whiplash they cannot name.

## Watch for

- An ID whose closing question gets folded back into a story before the counterpart answers. The I-primary has overridden the D-secondary at the wrong moment.
- Cooperative interruption rate dropping late. Usually the counterpart has gone passive rather than agreed.
- Repair attempts to ruptures the ID created with a fast close. The ID may be patching damage they could have prevented by slowing the close.

## Coaching move

Introduce a hold-the-silence practice at the close. After the closing question, the ID counts to five before speaking again, even if the silence feels uncomfortable. Watch the next session for whether the counterpart names a real condition in that gap. If they do, the room has been won and the deal has been negotiated, not just absorbed.
