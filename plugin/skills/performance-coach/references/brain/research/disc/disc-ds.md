# DISC Style: DS / Driver-Steady

Session-observed communication style guidance. Not personality destiny. People flex across contexts. Verify across multiple interactions.

## Headline

A DS drives the team forward without leaving anyone behind. They are the team member most likely to close a decision and check that everyone on the call actually agreed to it.

## In conversation

DS speakers carry a Driver's talk-share peak during decision windows, but the S-secondary slows their response latency just enough to let other voices land before they respond. Question architecture stays in closed and challenge territory at the close, but in the middle of the meeting more confirming and clarifying forms appear: "did I hear you say," "are we agreed that." Interruption profile is mostly cooperative; competitive interruptions are rare and almost always reserved for moments when the team is genuinely off the agreed plan. Topic-shift authorship is low; DS speakers stay with a topic until it is finished, where a pure D might pre-emptively cut to the next. Lexical entrainment (LSM) is moderate and grows across the call; the S-secondary holds the entrainment once it locks in. Repair attempts are noticeably more frequent than a pure D's, especially after challenge questions.

## Team contribution

DS speakers are the team's most stable internal closers. They are most valuable in long-cycle relationships, executive standing meetings, and any team where the cost of damaging trust to land a decision exceeds the value of the decision itself. Without a DS, teams with strong D-energy often hit the target this quarter and lose a teammate to the side-effects next quarter.

## Allowable weakness

DS speakers can be slower to escalate than a pure D, and the S-secondary makes them absorb friction they could otherwise have surfaced cleanly. They can carry frustration privately for long stretches before naming it, which means when they do escalate it can land harder than the situation warranted.

## Watch for

- A DS whose response latency stretches well past their personal baseline. Often a tell that they are absorbing rather than processing.
- Repair attempts after every challenge question. The S-secondary can be apologizing for the D's own appropriate behavior.
- Talk share dropping during conflict. The DS may be moderating themselves out of a moment that needed them.

## Coaching move

Introduce a name-the-friction practice at the close of every standing meeting where the DS leads. They explicitly surface one piece of friction they noticed during the meeting and what they did with it. Watch the next session for whether the team handles the friction live rather than carrying it home. If they do, the S-secondary is being used as awareness and not as suppression.
