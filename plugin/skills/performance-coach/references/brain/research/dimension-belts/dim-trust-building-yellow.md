# Trust Building at Yellow Depth

Belt bars are whole-rapport only ([[belt-system]]). This note is coaching language for depth of the **trust building** dimension near the yellow performance band. Do not award a per-dimension belt.

## Headline

Yellow Belt Trust Building means the other person cooperates, commitments land as made, and transparency is present when it costs nothing.

## In conversation

Yellow Belt Trust Building registers through commitment-signal density: language like "I'll have that to you by Thursday" appears above the session median and holds over time. Transparency is present in low-cost windows. When reasoning is easy to share, they share it.

What is not yet happening is a measurable shift in the other person's language. The other party's question-asking rate stays roughly steady rather than climbing; that climb is the signal that would indicate psychological safety taking hold. Hedging frequency in the other person's turns does not drop appreciably from opening to close.

Their talk share stays consistent across high-stakes and low-stakes segments; they do not compress their own space when the room gets hard, nor expand it to hold the situation together. The steadiness is present; the other person's talk share trajectory is what's missing at Yellow Belt.

Recovery from friction is passive: the practitioner lets the moment pass rather than naming it.

## Team contribution

A Yellow Belt Trust Building member is reliable in the operational sense. Colleagues know what to expect. Their commitment-signal density creates a low-friction baseline: nobody has to track their promises because they track them. Customers read them as credible and meetings tend to produce clear next steps.

## Allowable weakness

The trust they build is transactional. It rests on demonstrated competence and follow-through, which is real, but it does not reach the level where the other person brings the difficult thing. A customer with a concern that might jeopardize the deal does not surface it. The Yellow Belt practitioner is trusted to execute, not yet trusted as the person you go to when you need to say the real thing.

## Watch for

- The other party's question-asking rate stays flat or declines. In a trust-growing conversation, questions should increase as comfort increases.
- Transparency appears only when the stakes are low. If the practitioner goes quiet the moment sharing their reasoning would create friction, the pattern is conditional.
- Follow-through on large commitments is strong but small social commitments are inconsistently tracked.

## Coaching move

The Yellow-to-Blue transition requires a completed roleplay session at Yellow Belt level and demonstrated transparency under mild pressure, not just when it is free. The practice is a transparency count: across five real conversations, mark every moment where sharing your reasoning would create minor awkwardness and note whether you share it anyway. In roleplay, practice the moment where your counterpart has a question you do not have a clean answer to. The Blue Belt response names the uncertainty directly. Proof the practice is landing is a moment where the other person asks a question they would not have asked before.
