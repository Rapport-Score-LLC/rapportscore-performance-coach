# Emotional Acknowledgment at Yellow Depth

Belt bars are whole-rapport only ([[belt-system]]). This note is coaching language for depth of the **emotional acknowledgment** dimension near the yellow performance band. Do not award a per-dimension belt.

## Headline

Yellow Belt Emotional Acknowledgment means the acknowledgment is there, and it lands, but it arrives after the other person has already done the work of naming what they feel.

## In conversation

At Yellow Belt, acknowledgment is concentrated on explicit emotional statements: moments where the other person has already named the feeling. The practitioner mirrors back what was stated ("I can hear that this has been frustrating") rather than naming what has appeared but not yet been spoken.

RapportScore tracks acknowledgment timing as the interval between when a sentiment shift first registers and when the practitioner responds. At Yellow that interval spans one full turn. Response latency after acknowledgment is not yet deliberate: the practitioner names the emotion and advances to content in the same breath, treating acknowledgment as a courtesy beat. Talk share does not drop in turns following high-emotion moments, limiting how much room the other person actually uses. Acknowledgment-to-bypass ratio is meaningful but not consistent: clear signals get acknowledged; subtle ones, including tone shifts and hedging language, are still bypassed at a rate that registers as a gap at the next level.

## Team contribution

A Yellow Belt member signals that what is felt in the room is not invisible. Their willingness to acknowledge stated emotions raises the floor: conversations are less likely to derail because an unaddressed feeling accumulated pressure. Buyers notice when their stated concern is reflected back, and that noticing reduces friction even when the concern is not yet resolved.

## Allowable weakness

Yellow Belt does not yet access the conversations beneath the conversation. Emotional undercurrents that most people never name directly continue to move underground, and in negotiations the real concern is often the one that never gets surfaced. Returning to content quickly after acknowledging can leave the other person feeling heard in a technical sense but not fully understood.

## Watch for

- Acknowledging only after the other person has repeated the concern a second time, suggesting the first signal was bypassed.
- Acknowledgments followed immediately by agenda pivots with no beat in between.
- High sentiment variance on the other person's side late in calls, indicating unacknowledged content accumulated rather than dissipated.

## Coaching move

The Yellow-to-Blue transition requires the practitioner to begin acknowledging signals the other person has not named aloud. In the next three recorded conversations, find two moments where sentiment analysis registered a shift but the other person did not label it, then name it in the moment ("I'm sensing some hesitation, am I reading that right?"). The roleplay session at Yellow Belt must include a scenario where emotional content is embedded in subtext; passing that scenario is Gate 2 for Blue Belt. The advancement marker is three consecutive calls where acknowledgment timing shows the label appearing before the other person restates the concern.
