# Curiosity at Yellow Depth

Belt bars are whole-rapport only ([[belt-system]]). This note is coaching language for depth of the **curiosity** dimension near the yellow performance band. Do not award a per-dimension belt.

## Headline

Yellow Belt Curiosity means questions are present and functional, but they primarily serve as setup for expertise rather than as genuine exploration of the other person's world.

## In conversation

At Yellow Belt, the question-to-assertion ratio hovers near 1:1: for every question asked, an assertion or piece of advice follows within one or two turns. Questions at this level are predominantly information-gathering in structure: timeline, budget, decision authority, next steps, what happened. These are closed or lightly open questions that seek facts rather than meaning. Follow-up questions appear, but they tend to jump to the next item on a mental checklist rather than pick up the thread of what was just said.

After a question lands, the pause before the other person responds is brief. When silence extends past the comfortable window, they fill it with a follow-up or a reframe before the original question has had time to work. Talk share during question-mode segments is roughly equal to assertion-mode segments, consistent with the 1:1 ratio.

Curiosity here is real, but it functions instrumentally. The questions are good; they are just pointed at gathering enough data to move to the professional's primary mode, which is offering perspective.

## Team contribution

A Yellow Belt Curiosity member ensures the team does not go into high-stakes conversations blind. They gather the baseline information that grounds decision-making: timelines, constraints, stakeholder maps. They bring the discipline of asking before concluding, even if they do not yet sustain that discipline deep into the conversation.

## Allowable weakness

Information questions reveal what is happening on the surface; they do not surface what the situation means to the person experiencing it. The expertise pivot arrives too early, closing down lines of thinking that would have led somewhere useful.

## Watch for

- The expertise pivot appearing in the second turn after a question: ask, hear the answer, immediately shift to recommendation.
- Questions following a fixed sequence regardless of what the other person said.
- Silence or a change in the other person's engagement after the expertise pivot, signaling they had more to say.

## Coaching move

The Yellow-to-Blue transition is built on one practice: the double follow-up rule. Before offering any assertion or recommendation after a question, require at least two follow-up questions that stay on the thread of what the other person just said. The second follow-up should go one layer deeper, asking about meaning or significance rather than additional facts. In the next roleplay session, the reviewer looks for this pattern to appear at least three times and checks whether follow-ups stay on the prior answer's thread or jump to a new topic. A Blue Belt score requires sustaining the ratio above 2:1 across consecutive calls, and the double follow-up discipline is the behavioral mechanism that produces that ratio.
