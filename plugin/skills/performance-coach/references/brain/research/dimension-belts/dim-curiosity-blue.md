# Curiosity at Blue Depth

Belt bars are whole-rapport only ([[belt-system]]). This note is coaching language for depth of the **curiosity** dimension near the blue performance band. Do not award a per-dimension belt.

## Headline

Blue Belt Curiosity means the professional has broken the one-question-then-advice reflex and is beginning to stay in the conversation long enough to hear what is beneath the surface answer.

## In conversation

The question-to-assertion ratio at Blue Belt clears 2:1 in high-value conversations: two or more questions for every assertion or recommendation offered. The shift from Yellow Belt is not just in quantity. The question mix changes: alongside information questions, exploration questions begin to appear. "What was that like for you?" after a factual exchange, "what made you decide to go that direction?" after someone describes a choice. These are questions about experience and meaning, not just facts. Follow-up questions stay on the prior answer's thread rather than jumping to the next checklist item.

Talk share in question-mode segments falls below the session average, a measurable shift from Yellow Belt's 1:1 pattern. The practitioner is genuinely talking less when curiosity is activated. Expertise, when it does appear, has softened: "I wonder if..." replaces "what you should do is."

## Team contribution

A Blue Belt Curiosity member surfaces meaning, not just facts: why the other person made the choice they made, what the constraint actually costs them, what they have not yet resolved for themselves. In sales contexts this uncovers the real buying motive behind the stated requirement. The Blue Belt member's conversations produce richer raw material for the team to work with.

## Allowable weakness

The 2:1 ratio holds in comfortable territory and slips under pressure, which is precisely when it matters most. Exploration questions can feel like a practice being applied rather than a natural operating mode. Blue Belt Curiosity is real, but it is not yet the professional's default gear.

## Watch for

- The ratio compressing toward 1:1 during objection handling or pushback, signaling Blue Belt habits have not transferred to challenging contexts.
- Exploration questions following a fixed pattern regardless of what the other person just said.
- A front-loaded ratio where questions concentrate in the opening phase and the second half reverts to assertion.

## Coaching move

The Blue-to-Purple transition is built on two practices. The first is implication questions: after an exploration question lands, the next follow-up asks what that answer means for the situation being navigated. "What does that tell you about what needs to happen next?" is an implication question. "And how long has that been going on?" is another information question. Roleplay sessions at Blue Belt should include at least two implication question sequences, and reviewers check whether questions respond to what was said or follow a predetermined arc. The second practice is holding the discomfort: when the urge to offer expertise is strongest, commit to one more question first. Purple Belt requires a 3:1 ratio sustained across three to five consecutive calls.
