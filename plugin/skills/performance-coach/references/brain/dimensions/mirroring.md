# Mirroring

One of the seven Rapport Dimensions. Mirroring measures how effectively you match the other person's communication style, pace, and tone. Matching builds subconscious connection and makes others feel understood.

## Why it matters

Matching the other person's language and pace builds common ground without saying a word about it, especially under pressure.

## The observable behavior

Reuse their exact key words in your reply instead of swapping in your own vocabulary.

## Transfer plan (from your coaching path)

- **Do**: repeat the last two or three important words they said, as a question.
- **When**: when they push back, hesitate, or raise a concern.
- **Proof**: your Mirroring score holds instead of dropping during objections.

## How to read your score

Scored 0-100 on the Trust and Rapport radar; gates belts at the shared bar (see [[belt-system]]). Mirroring is style matching, not content parroting: the measurements below track convergence in function words, pace, and structure.

## Related measurements

- [[language-style-matching]]: function-word alignment (LSM), the core mirroring signal, with an over-time timeline.
- [[entrainment]]: the four-component convergence composite (style, content adoption, structure, hedge convergence).
- [[speech-rate-modulation]]: whether your speaking rates converge across the call.

## Drills

- Echo-as-question: repeat their last two or three key words with rising intonation, then wait. See [[drills-library]].
- Ten-second cadence read: listen to their pace for ten seconds, then match it before making your point.

## FAQ

**Is mirroring manipulative?**
The measured behavior is attentiveness made visible: adopting shared vocabulary and pace so the other person feels understood. It happens naturally in good conversations; the score tells you whether it happened in yours.

**Does mirroring mean agreeing?**
No. You can mirror language and pace while holding a different position. In fact holding your position calmly while staying matched is a strong combination during objections (see [[objection-structure]]).
