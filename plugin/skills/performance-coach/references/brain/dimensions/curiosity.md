# Curiosity

One of the seven Rapport Dimensions. Curiosity measures how well you demonstrate genuine interest through thoughtful questions and exploration. Good questions lead to deeper understanding.

## Why it matters

Open questions surface the real priorities that decide outcomes. Telling feels productive; asking actually is.

## The observable behavior

Ask one more open question before you offer a solution.

## Transfer plan (from your coaching path)

- **Do**: ask a "what" or "how" follow-up instead of presenting.
- **When**: the first time you feel ready to pitch or explain.
- **Proof**: your Curiosity score rises on your next analyzed call.

## How to read your score

Scored 0-100 on the Trust and Rapport radar; gates belts at the shared bar (see [[belt-system]]). Curiosity is evidenced by question behavior: how many questions, how open, and whether follow-ups chain off what was just said.

## Related measurements

- [[questions-asked]]: question types (open versus closed) and follow-up chains, the direct evidence base.
- [[qa-adequacy]]: whether the questions you asked actually got answered.
- [[agency-orientation]]: your asked-versus-answered balance, which shows whether you drove the questioning.
- [[talk-time-and-turns]]: high talk time with low question rate is the classic explaining-instead-of-exploring shape.

## Drills

- One question, then silence: ask a single open question and let the silence work. Never stack a second question on top. See [[drills-library]].
- The cost question: when a pain word appears ("burned", "frustrating", "expensive"), ask what it cost them before explaining anything.
- Q&A pattern guidance from the product: aim for a 3:1 open-to-closed question ratio (Questions and Answers card).

## FAQ

**Are closed questions bad?**
No. Closed questions get specific information; open questions invite dialogue. The product's guidance is a 3:1 open-to-closed aim, not zero closed questions.

**I asked many questions and still scored low. Why?**
Stacked questions (two or more in one turn) usually get only the last one answered, and rhetorical questions are not curiosity. See the falsifiers in [[questions-asked]].
