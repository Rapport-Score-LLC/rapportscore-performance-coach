# Interruptions

## What it measures

Times a speaker began talking while another speaker was mid-turn, counted per direction (who interrupted whom) and paired with yields (how often the interrupted speaker gave up the floor). Backchannels, the short supportive tokens under about a second, are excluded so active listening never counts as interrupting. Research anchor: interruption and overlap as dominance versus collaboration in conversation research (Tannen 1984).

## Where you see it

The Power Dynamics card, the Interruptions sub-table in All Parties LSM and Dynamics (who interrupted whom, and yields), and the Interrupts and Received columns in the Conversation Network Signature card.

## How to read your number

- No published universal threshold: a few overlaps are normal in animated discussion. Compare against your own history and the call type.
- Direction is the signal: many interruptions in one direction with none received often signals urgency, frustration, or dominance. A speaker who interrupts often but rarely yields is driving the conversation.
- Not all overlaps are hostile: some are collaborative completions ("yes, and..."), some are cut-offs. The coach pairs the count with the emotional context before reading it as a problem.
- Your yields matter as much as your interruptions: yielding 8 of 9 interruptions within two seconds is a floor-holding pattern worth coaching in itself.

## What moves it

- Down (your interruptions): letting silence do the work after their sentences, noting your point instead of launching it, waiting for the natural turn end.
- Up (holding your floor): finishing your sentence calmly when overlapped, re-entering once after being cut ("to finish the thought...").
- Falsifiers the coach applies before naming a pattern: backchannels never count; simultaneous restarts after a mutual silence never count; sessions the platform flags for audio lag are read cautiously.

## Drills

- Hold the floor: on your next call, finish one overlapped sentence calmly instead of yielding, then hand the turn back deliberately. See [[drills-library]].
- The re-entry: after being cut off, one calm re-entry ("before we move on, the last piece was...").

## FAQ

**Are interruptions always bad?**
No. Higher interruption counts can show engagement, and collaborative overlaps build energy. The pattern worth coaching is one-directional cutting or reflexive yielding.

**Someone interrupts me constantly. Can the coach fix them?**
The coach works the side of the net you control: your re-entries, your floor-holding, your pacing. Your own behavior in those rooms is observable and trainable; verdicts about the other person are not on offer.
