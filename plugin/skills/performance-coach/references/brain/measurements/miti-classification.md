# MITI Classification

Companion key documented here: `reflective_listening` (reflection coding per the same manual).

## What it measures

Every focus-speaker turn classified into a behavioral code from MITI 4.2.1 (Motivational Interviewing Treatment Integrity), adapted for business conversations: Simple Reflection, Complex Reflection, Affirm, Emphasize Autonomy, Seek Collaboration, Giving Information, Questions, plus two non-adherent codes, Persuade and Confront. From the codes it derives three ratios and reads the other side's language (change talk versus sustain talk). It is not a certified clinical rating; it is the coding framework applied to your transcript. Research anchor: the MITI 4.2.1 coding manual (Moyers, Manuel and Ernst 2014). The companion key `reflective_listening` carries the reflection coding itself.

## Where you see it

The MITI 4.2.1 Classification card: proficiency badge, three ratio tiles, a behavioral-code bar chart, client-language distribution, and warnings when non-adherent behaviors appear.

## How to read your number

- **R:Q Ratio** (reflections divided by questions): target above 1.0. Below 1.0 means you asked more than you reflected, a common discovery-phase gap.
- **% Complex Reflections** (complex divided by total reflections): target above 40%. Below it, most of your reflections parroted rather than added meaning (see [[complex-reflections]]).
- **Adherence Index** (adherent reflections minus twice the non-adherent count): target above 0. The formula deliberately penalizes Persuade and Confront more than it rewards good moves; a positive score means the conversation was net consistent with the method.
- Proficiency badge: ADVANCED requires R:Q at or above 1.0 AND %CR at or above 40%. DEVELOPING is the starting point, not a failure; most practitioners stay there until they deliberately train complex reflections.
- Coverage warning: if fewer than 80% of turns were classified, treat all ratios as directional for that session.
- Client language: more than 50% change-favorable talk from the other side is flagged as a strong signal.

## What moves it

- Swapping a question for a reflection: after they explain something, reflect it before asking anything.
- Cutting Persuade and Confront moves: pushing and confronting measurably increase resistance; even one or two instances lower the index and are worth finding in the transcript.
- Affirming and emphasizing autonomy: naming what they did well and what remains their call are counted adherent moves.

## Drills

- Reflection-first: for one call, respond to every explained problem with a reflection before any question or suggestion. Check R:Q next session. See [[drills-library]].
- Persuade hunt: find your Persuade turns in the last transcript and script the reflection you could have used instead.

## FAQ

**Why does a method from counseling apply to sales?**
It was designed for change conversations, and sales, coaching, and leadership are change conversations. The mechanics of resistance and motivation transfer directly.

**Is a negative Adherence Index one bad call or a problem?**
One session is directional. A consistently negative index across sessions means pushing is a pattern, and it is one of the most trainable patterns in the library.
