# Behavioral Styles (DISC, Social Style, Conflict Modes)

No single registry measurement key: these are session-measured communication-style surfaces on the Behavioral tab (a registry-record gap is logged in the vault's ledger). One rule governs all three: they describe observed communication behavior in this conversation, never personality, identity, or anything clinical. People flex styles across contexts; use these as guidance, not labels.

## What they measure

- **DISC** (each 0-100): Dominance (direct, results-focused), Influence (enthusiastic, people-focused), Steadiness (patient, consistency-preferring), Conscientiousness (analytical, accuracy-focused), read from observable patterns in the session.
- **Social Style**: position on two axes: Assertiveness (ask-oriented 0-50 versus tell-oriented 51-100) and Responsiveness (task-focused 0-50 versus openly expressive 51-100), yielding four quadrants: Analytical, Driver, Amiable, Expressive.
- **Conflict Modes** (Thomas-Kilmann): the observed balance across five approaches to disagreement: Competing, Collaborating, Compromising, Avoiding, Accommodating. Each has appropriate uses; most people have a primary mode and can adapt.

## Where you see them

The Behavioral tab of a session: DISC Profile, Social Style Matrix, and Conflict Style cards, with adaptation tips per profile.

## How to read them

- Session-scoped: the same person may show a different pattern in a different session. Based on one conversation, context affects behavior; verify across multiple interactions and treat any single read as hypothesis, not fact.
- Use for adaptation, not judgment. Card guidance: high-D reads want directness and results; high-I want enthusiasm and relationship; high-S want patience and stability; high-C want detail and evidence. Drivers want efficiency; Expressives want recognition; Amiables want harmony; Analyticals want accuracy.
- Conflict modes are situational tools: Competing fits urgent decisions, Collaborating fits important relationships, Compromising fits time-limited situations, Avoiding fits trivial issues or cooling off, Accommodating fits preserving harmony. Know your default and adapt.

## What moves them

Nothing to optimize: these are descriptive reads to adapt around, not scores to raise. The coachable skill is versatility: noticing the other side's measured style and flexing your pace, detail level, and emotional expression to meet it.

## Drills

- Style-matched primer: before a follow-up call, read the counterpart's last measured style and pick one concrete adaptation (brevity for a high-D read, detail for a high-C read). See [[drills-library]] and [[session-types]].

## FAQ

**Is this a personality test?**
No. It is observed communication behavior in a specific recorded session. No verdicts about who anyone IS are on offer, from this surface or any other.

**Can I ask the coach what type my buyer "really" is?**
The coach declines type-as-identity and serves the measured style instead: "this session profiled high-D communication on their side: short turns, decision language; match with brevity."
