# Questions Asked

Companion key documented here: `question_sequencing` (follow-up question chains).

## What it measures

`question_structure` classifies every question in the call by type (open, closed, rhetorical, and similar categories) and attributes it to its asker. `question_sequencing` measures follow-up chains: whether questions build on the prior answer to go deeper, the behavior behind real discovery. Units are counts, per-speaker, plus a type breakdown. Research anchors: dialogue-act question typology (Stolcke et al. 2000) and follow-up question research showing follow-ups build liking and information flow (Huang, Yeomans, Brooks, Minson and Gino 2017).

## Where you see it

The Questions and Answers card (all questions, who asked, how they were answered), the question-type badges and Questions Asked / Answers Given counts in Speaker Statistics, the Discovery toggle on the Communication Timeline (question-answer pairs and question chains), and the question rate in the Session Insight Summary.

## How to read your number

- Open questions ("what", "how", "why", "tell me", "walk me through") encourage dialogue; closed questions ("do", "is", "can", "did") get specific information. Both have jobs.
- Product guidance: aim for a 3:1 open-to-closed ratio (Questions and Answers card).
- Question clusters show discovery depth: chained follow-ups on one thread beat scattered singles.
- Coaching guidelines from the corner-coach anchor table (expert-estimate provenance, cited as correlations): 11-14 questions per discovery call with 4 or more open-ended.
- Falsifiers before any "low questions" verdict: rhetorical questions ("makes sense, right?") are excluded; clarifying restatements ("so Tuesday, not Thursday?") are confirmation, not curiosity; compliance-scripted question blocks do not count against you.

## What moves it

- Replacing "Did you...?" with "What has your experience been with...?" (Communication Overview guidance).
- Asking one question at a time: stacking 2 or more questions in one turn means prospects answer only the last, usually easiest, one.
- Following up on partial answers instead of moving on.
- Letting them ask too: an interrogation is not a conversation.

## Drills

- One question, then silence. The check is your open-ended count next session. See [[drills-library]].
- The cost question after pain words, before any explaining.
- Un-stack: when you catch a stacked question mid-call, pick one aloud ("the one I really want to ask is...").

## FAQ

**Which question did they answer when I stacked three?**
Almost always the last one, and usually the easiest. That is why stacking is coached even when the questions were individually good.

**Is a high question count enough for Curiosity?**
No. Type and sequencing carry the signal: open forms and chained follow-ups. See [[curiosity]].
