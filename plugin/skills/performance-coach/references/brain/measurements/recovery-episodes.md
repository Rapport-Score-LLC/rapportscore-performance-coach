# Recovery Episodes

## What it measures

Moments of conversational friction and what happened next. Each episode is a detected friction trigger (an alignment drop, a momentum stall, a cluster of repair attempts, or a long response gap) followed by an outcome: Recovered (the friction was repaired and momentum returned), Partial (some repair without full recovery), or Did not recover (the window passed without successful repair). Summary stats: total friction events, recovery rate, and average recovery time. Research anchors: rupture-and-repair research on working alliances (Safran and Muran) and complete recovery arcs in relationship research (Gottman).

## Where you see it

The Recovery Episodes card, alongside the Rapport Moments timeline (friction and recovery cluster together), and repair events on the Communication Timeline.

## How to read your number

- A high recovery rate means ruptures were caught and repaired in the moment: a strong rapport signal.
- Repeated "Did not recover" outcomes around the same trigger type point to a pattern worth coaching.
- Average recovery time shows how quickly momentum returned once a repair began.
- No published threshold for a "good" recovery rate: compare across your own sessions. Descriptive, never a verdict: it reflects observable conversational patterns, not intent.

## What moves it

- Noticing the dip: recoveries start with registering that something landed wrong (the other side went short, slow, or flat).
- A quotable repair move: an acknowledgment, a clarifying question, or a pace change in the 60 seconds after the dip. The coach only credits recoveries where the move that preceded the turn can be quoted; a rebound that merely happened nearby is not your recovery.
- What prevents recovery: topic-forcing past the dip, or silence that leaves the rupture standing.

## Drills

- The 22:10 review: find your last session's biggest dip, watch 30 seconds before and after, and name the exact move (or missing move). See [[drills-library]].
- One clarifying question at the first sign of friction on your next call, before any defense.

## FAQ

**Is friction itself a failure?**
No. Friction is normal in real conversations; unrepaired friction is the cost. Some of your best coaching tape is a clean recovery, and the coach names those precisely so you can repeat them.

**Can too many repairs be bad?**
A moderate repair rate is the hallmark of an adaptive communicator. Dense repairs in one stretch usually mean that segment was confusing; review what was being explained there (see [[repair-attempts]]).
