# Silence Mechanics

## What it measures

The pauses, gaps, and overlaps between turns: how long the silence lasts, whose turn it follows, and how it compares to each speaker's own baseline gap. Units are seconds and milliseconds. Research anchor: pause, gap, and overlap research in conversation phonetics (Heldner and Edlund 2010).

## Where you see it

Pause-gap markers on the Communication Timeline (the pill-shaped markers between bars: long pause, quick response, or normal, each relative to that speaker's baseline, hover for exact duration and ratio), the Patience card, silence analysis in the communication tiles, and structural silence events in [[timing-critical-moments]].

## How to read your number

- Timeline markers are baseline-relative: a long-pause marker means longer than that speaker's own typical gap, a quick-response marker means shorter. Normal means within their baseline range.
- Post-objection silence: 2 to 10 seconds of dead air immediately after an objection is detected structurally as a timing event. It is a moment worth reviewing, not automatically bad.
- A held silence after asking for a decision (post-close dwell) is detected as positive pacing.
- No single published threshold for "good silence": read gaps against the speaker's own baseline and the moment's stakes.

## What moves it

- Comfort with pausing instead of filling: the Communication Overview card's guidance is to pause silently instead of filling gaps, because silence builds authority where fillers spend it.
- The strategic pause: a deliberate 1.5-2.0 second hold after a high-stakes or emotional statement from the other side (coaching guideline from the corner-coach drill library, expert-estimate provenance). Its presence pairs with stronger emotional acknowledgment.
- What degrades it: crowding (habitually starting under 0.5s), and filling every gap with "um" or a restart (see [[speech-rate-modulation]] and the Delivery Confidence card for fillers).

## Drills

- The two-second gift: hold 1.5-2.0 seconds after any big statement before responding. See [[drills-library]].
- One naked pause per call: after asking your most important question, say nothing until they answer.

## FAQ

**Their long pause after my price: good or bad?**
It is information. Post-objection and post-ask silences often mean processing. The coach reads what came immediately before and after rather than scoring the silence alone.

**Are all overlaps interruptions?**
No. Short supportive tokens under about a second ("right", "mm-hm") are backchannels, excluded from interruption counts. See [[backchannel]] and [[interruptions]].
