# Complex Reflections

## What it measures

When a speaker reflects a partner's prior content with added meaning: paraphrase, inference, emotion expansion, or causal linkage, as opposed to a simple echo of the same words. Per speaker it counts Complex, Simple, and Question-form reflections plus the Complex-to-Simple ratio. It also tracks signal diversity: how many of the five added-meaning sub-signals (hypothesis marker, causal connective, emotion expansion, abstraction shift, stake elevation) appeared across your complex reflections. Research anchor: the simple-versus-complex reflection codes of the MITI 4.2.1 coding manual (Moyers et al. 2014).

## Where you see it

The Complex Reflections card, with a per-reflection list, a pairwise matrix for multi-party calls (who reflects whose content), and the related ratios on the [[miti-classification]] card.

## How to read your number

- The Complex-to-Simple ratio is the highest-signal aggregate. The MITI expert threshold is 1.0 or above (more complex than simple).
- Classification logic, stated openly: a reflection counts as Complex when it is semantically about the prior turn (similarity at or above 0.55), uses substantially different words (lexical overlap at or below 0.30), and fires at least one of the five added-meaning signals. High similarity with high overlap is a simple echo. Reflections ending in a question mark are counted separately as question reflections. Self-reflections of your own prior content do not count.
- These thresholds are placeholders pending calibration against trained MITI coders; the product says so on the card. V1 is research-grade: for coaching only, never for hiring, firing, evaluation, or any decision affecting a person.
- A consistently low ratio with low signal diversity is the novice pattern; a high ratio with diverse signals is the expert active-listening pattern.

## What moves it

- Paraphrasing with your own words plus one added element: the implied feeling, the likely cause, the stake ("so if this slips again, the renewal is what's really at risk").
- Avoiding parrot mode: repeating their exact sentence back adds no meaning and stays classified simple.
- Reflecting before asking: a reflection followed by a question outperforms question stacks for depth.

## Drills

- One complex reflection per call: after they explain a problem, reflect it back in different words plus one inference, then pause. Verify your ratio next session. See [[drills-library]].

## FAQ

**Is a simple reflection worthless?**
No. Simple reflections still prove tracking. The ratio just rewards the stronger move: adding meaning is what makes people feel deeply understood.

**What does the "coaching suspect" flag mean?**
A reflection that arrived unusually slowly for you while firing all five added-meaning signals at once is flagged as anomalous, possibly assisted rather than native skill. It is a data-quality marker, not an accusation.
